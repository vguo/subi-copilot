# Internal Medicine Handoff Voice Memo Scripts

These are fictional internal medicine handoff scripts designed for training a sub-I handoff agent. Each script is approximately one minute spoken and includes a fake patient name, fake bed location, one-liner, high-level events from the day, overnight watch-outs, contingencies, code status, and likely disposition.

---

## 1. COPD Exacerbation

**Patient:** Maria Lopez  
**Location:** 7 East, Bed 12

Maria Lopez is a 67-year-old woman in 7 East Bed 12 with COPD on home two liters, admitted for COPD exacerbation likely triggered by viral URI. Today she was clinically improved. We spaced her duonebs from every four hours to every six hours, continued prednisone day three of five, and continued azithromycin. She was weaned from four liters to two liters nasal cannula, which is her baseline. Her chest X-ray did not show pneumonia, and her procalcitonin was low, so no broad-spectrum antibiotics were started.

Overnight, please watch her respiratory status and oxygen requirement. Goal sat is 88 to 92 percent. If she has increased work of breathing, rising oxygen needs, or altered mental status, please evaluate at bedside and consider VBG, chest X-ray, and escalating nebs. She is full code. Anticipate discharge tomorrow if stable on home oxygen.

## 2. Heart Failure Exacerbation

**Patient:** James Patel  
**Location:** 6 West, Bed 4

James Patel is a 74-year-old man in 6 West Bed 4 with HFrEF, EF 30 percent, CKD stage 3, and hypertension, admitted for acute decompensated heart failure. Today he received IV Lasix 80 milligrams twice with good urine output, about two liters net negative so far. His breathing is improved, but he still has bibasilar crackles and lower extremity edema. His creatinine bumped slightly from 1.6 to 1.8, but electrolytes were okay this afternoon. We held his home lisinopril because of the AKI and continued carvedilol.

Overnight, please monitor urine output, blood pressure, and oxygen requirement. If he becomes hypotensive, hold further diuresis and evaluate. If he becomes more short of breath, consider additional IV Lasix if blood pressure and creatinine allow. Please replete potassium if less than 4 and magnesium if less than 2. He is DNR/DNI but okay for ICU-level care.

## 3. Community-Acquired Pneumonia

**Patient:** Linda Chen  
**Location:** 8 North, Bed 21

Linda Chen is a 58-year-old woman in 8 North Bed 21 with type 2 diabetes and asthma, admitted for community-acquired pneumonia with hypoxemia. She came in requiring three liters nasal cannula and is now down to one liter. Today she remained afebrile, white count improved, and blood cultures are no growth to date. We continued ceftriaxone and doxycycline, day two. She tolerated a regular diet and worked with PT, who recommended home with services.

Overnight, please watch for fever, increasing oxygen requirement, or worsening respiratory symptoms. If she spikes a fever or becomes unstable, repeat blood cultures, get a chest X-ray, and consider broadening antibiotics depending on appearance. Her goal oxygen saturation is above 92 percent. She has PRN albuterol ordered for wheezing. She is full code. If stable overnight, she may be ready to transition to oral antibiotics tomorrow.

## 4. GI Bleed

**Patient:** Robert Williams  
**Location:** 5 South, Bed 9

Robert Williams is a 69-year-old man in 5 South Bed 9 with atrial fibrillation on apixaban and history of peptic ulcer disease, admitted for melena and symptomatic anemia. His apixaban is being held. Today his hemoglobin was stable at 8.1 after one unit of packed red cells overnight. GI saw him and plans for EGD tomorrow morning. He is NPO after midnight. He is on IV pantoprazole twice daily. No further melena was reported today, and vitals have been stable.

Overnight, please watch for recurrent melena, hematemesis, tachycardia, hypotension, or dizziness. If unstable, please evaluate immediately, send stat CBC, type and screen if needed, and call GI. Transfusion threshold is hemoglobin less than 7, or higher if active bleeding or instability. Do not restart anticoagulation overnight. He is full code.

## 5. Sepsis from UTI

**Patient:** Angela Brooks  
**Location:** 7 West, Bed 18

Angela Brooks is an 82-year-old woman in 7 West Bed 18 with dementia and recurrent UTIs, admitted for sepsis from presumed urinary source. She initially had fever, tachycardia, leukocytosis, and positive urinalysis. Today she was more alert and afebrile. Urine culture is growing E. coli, sensitivities pending. Blood cultures are no growth so far. We continued ceftriaxone, day two, and gave maintenance fluids earlier, now stopped because she is eating and drinking better. Her creatinine improved from 1.4 to 1.1.

Overnight, please watch mental status, fever curve, blood pressure, and urine output. If she becomes hypotensive or febrile again, please assess volume status, consider repeat lactate and blood cultures, and broaden antibiotics if clinically worsening. She can get acetaminophen for fever. She is at high delirium risk, so avoid sedating meds if possible. Code status is DNR/DNI.

## 6. DKA, Now Improving

**Patient:** Marcus Johnson  
**Location:** Stepdown Unit, Bed 3

Marcus Johnson is a 42-year-old man in Stepdown Bed 3 with type 1 diabetes, admitted with DKA after missing insulin. His anion gap closed this morning, and he was transitioned off the insulin drip to subcutaneous glargine with mealtime lispro. He is now tolerating a carb-consistent diet. Endocrine was consulted and recommended glargine 24 units nightly plus lispro 6 units with meals and correction scale. His potassium was 3.7 this afternoon and was repleted.

Overnight, please watch fingersticks closely and make sure he receives basal insulin even if not eating. If glucose is persistently above 300, please check BMP and consider whether the gap is reopening. If he has nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, or altered mental status, please evaluate and repeat labs. Also replete potassium if low. He is full code. Likely transfer to floor tomorrow if stable.

## 7. Cirrhosis with Ascites

**Patient:** Samuel Rivera  
**Location:** 6 East, Bed 16

Samuel Rivera is a 61-year-old man in 6 East Bed 16 with alcohol-associated cirrhosis complicated by ascites and prior hepatic encephalopathy, admitted for abdominal distension and mild confusion. Today he had a diagnostic and therapeutic paracentesis with four liters removed. Fluid studies were not consistent with SBP. He received albumin after the tap. His mental status improved on lactulose, and he had three bowel movements today. We continued spironolactone and furosemide.

Overnight, please watch for worsening confusion, abdominal pain, fever, hypotension, or bleeding from the paracentesis site. Goal is two to three bowel movements daily; if no bowel movement overnight and he seems more confused, please give PRN lactulose. Avoid benzodiazepines and sedating medications. If he develops fever or severe abdominal pain, consider repeat evaluation for infection and call the senior. He is full code.

Her initial ECG showed no ST elevations, and serial troponins have been negative so far. Cardiology saw her today and recommended continued telemetry and nuclear stress test tomorrow. She has been chest pain-free since this morning. We continued aspirin, atorvastatin, and her home metoprolol. She is NPO after midnight for the stress test.

Overnight, please watch for recurrent chest pain, shortness of breath, diaphoresis, or telemetry changes. If chest pain recurs, please get a stat ECG and troponin, give nitroglycerin if blood pressure allows, and notify cardiology if concerning. Avoid caffeine overnight because of the planned stress test. She is full code. If stress test is negative, she may discharge tomorrow.

## 9. Cellulitis

**Patient:** Thomas Nguyen  
**Location:** 9 South, Bed 2

Thomas Nguyen is a 63-year-old man in 9 South Bed 2 with diabetes and peripheral vascular disease, admitted for right lower extremity cellulitis. Today the erythema was slightly improved compared with the marked borders, and he remained afebrile. We continued IV cefazolin, day two. Blood cultures are negative so far. Doppler ultrasound was negative for DVT. His glucose was running high, so we increased his sliding-scale insulin and restarted his home basal insulin at a reduced dose.

Overnight, please watch for fever, hypotension, rapidly spreading erythema, worsening pain, bullae, crepitus, or pain out of proportion. If any of those occur, please evaluate urgently and consider imaging and broadening antibiotics for nec fasc concern. Otherwise, continue leg elevation and antibiotics. He has acetaminophen ordered for pain and fever. He is full code. Possible switch to oral antibiotics in one to two days.

## 10. Acute Kidney Injury

**Patient:** Eleanor Martin  
**Location:** 5 East, Bed 14

Eleanor Martin is a 79-year-old woman in 5 East Bed 14 with hypertension and CKD stage 3, admitted for AKI likely from dehydration after several days of poor oral intake and diarrhea. Today her creatinine improved from 2.4 to 1.9 after IV fluids. Diarrhea has slowed, C. diff testing was negative, and she is tolerating oral intake. We stopped maintenance fluids this afternoon to avoid volume overload. Her home losartan and hydrochlorothiazide are being held.

Overnight, please monitor urine output, blood pressure, and any recurrent diarrhea or vomiting. If she becomes hypotensive or has poor intake, consider a small fluid bolus after assessing volume status. Avoid nephrotoxins and do not restart her ARB or thiazide overnight. Morning BMP is ordered. She is DNR but okay for intubation and ICU escalation. If kidney function continues to improve, she may discharge in the next day or two.

# Example PDF Checklist Format

For each patient, the agent could generate a one-page or multi-patient checklist like this:

```text
[ ] Confirm patient identity and location
[ ] Review one-liner and active problems
[ ] Review day events
[ ] Check overnight watch-outs
[ ] Confirm contingency plan
[ ] Confirm code status
[ ] Review pending labs, imaging, procedures, or consult recommendations
[ ] Identify discharge or escalation plan
```
