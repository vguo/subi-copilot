## 1. COPD Exacerbation

**Patient:** Maria Lopez  
**Location:** 7 East, Bed 12

### Anticipated overnight events

- She is likely to remain near her baseline 2 L nasal cannula requirement if the daytime improvement continues.
- Mild dyspnea or wheezing may recur as duonebs were spaced from q4h to q6h.
- Oxygen saturations may look "low" by non-COPD standards; goal is 88-92%, not routine normalization above 94%.
- Cough and sputum may persist overnight despite improvement; absence of pneumonia on CXR and low procalcitonin make bacterial deterioration less likely but still worth monitoring.
- If stable overnight, main overnight goal is avoiding unnecessary escalation and preserving discharge readiness for tomorrow.

### Medication-specific monitoring

- **Duonebs q6h:** watch for recurrent wheezing before next scheduled dose, tachycardia, tremor, or palpitations after treatments.
- **Prednisone day 3/5:** monitor for hyperglycemia, insomnia, mood changes, and worsening agitation/confusion.
- **Azithromycin:** monitor for GI upset and QT-risk if telemetry or other QT-prolonging meds are present.
- **Supplemental oxygen:** avoid over-oxygenation; target SpO2 88-92% because she has COPD on home oxygen.
- **No broad-spectrum antibiotics:** if new fever or focal infiltrate develops, reassess rather than assuming current regimen covers pneumonia.

### Decision triggers

- **If SpO2 < 88% persistently on 2 L NC**, increase oxygen stepwise, assess at bedside, and check work of breathing.
- **If oxygen need rises above 4 L NC or increases by >2 L from baseline**, evaluate at bedside and consider VBG, CXR, and more frequent bronchodilators.
- **If new altered mental status, somnolence, or severe headache occurs**, assess for hypercapnia and obtain VBG/ABG per local practice.
- **If respiratory rate > 28, accessory muscle use, inability to speak full sentences, or worsening wheeze despite treatment**, call senior/resident and consider escalation to continuous nebs, BiPAP evaluation, or higher level of care.
- **If fever, new focal lung findings, or hemodynamic instability develops**, repeat infectious evaluation and consider whether pneumonia coverage is now needed.

## 2. Heart Failure Exacerbation

**Patient:** James Patel  
**Location:** 6 West, Bed 4

### Anticipated overnight events

- He may continue to diurese significantly after two daytime IV Lasix 80 mg doses.
- Dyspnea should continue improving, but he remains volume overloaded with crackles and edema.
- Creatinine may rise further overnight or on morning labs given CKD3 and aggressive diuresis.
- Potassium and magnesium may fall after IV loop diuresis.
- Borderline hypotension may occur if he is intravascularly depleted despite residual edema.

### Medication-specific monitoring

- **IV Lasix 80 mg BID:** track urine output, net I/O, BP, dizziness, creatinine, potassium, and magnesium.
- **Carvedilol:** monitor for bradycardia, hypotension, and worsening fatigue/lightheadedness.
- **Held lisinopril:** do not restart overnight while creatinine is rising; reassess after renal trend stabilizes.
- **Electrolyte repletion:** maintain K > 4 and Mg > 2 to reduce arrhythmia risk in HFrEF.
- **Oxygen therapy if needed:** increasing O2 requirement may mean persistent pulmonary edema, not necessarily infection.

### Decision triggers

- **If SBP < 90, MAP < 65, symptomatic hypotension, or new dizziness/syncope**, hold further diuresis, evaluate volume status, and call senior.
- **If urine output < 0.5 mL/kg/hr for 4-6 hours or very low output despite Lasix**, assess bladder/retention, volume status, and renal function.
- **If oxygen requirement increases by >2 L, RR > 28, or worsening crackles/orthopnea**, evaluate for pulmonary edema and consider additional IV Lasix only if BP and renal status allow.
- **If K < 4 or Mg < 2**, replete per protocol and consider telemetry review.
- **If creatinine rises substantially from 1.8 on morning BMP or patient appears intravascularly dry**, discuss holding further diuresis and reassessing plan.

## 6. DKA, Now Improving

**Patient:** Marcus Johnson  
**Location:** Stepdown Unit, Bed 3

### Anticipated overnight events

- He should remain stable after transition to subcutaneous insulin if basal insulin is administered on schedule.
- Hyperglycemia may recur overnight after stopping insulin drip, especially if meal coverage was missed or intake changes.
- Potassium may fall with ongoing insulin therapy after afternoon repletion.
- Nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, or altered mental status would raise concern for recurrent ketosis/gap reopening.
- If stable overnight, he may transfer from stepdown to the floor tomorrow.

### Medication-specific monitoring

- **Glargine 24 units nightly:** must be given even if not eating to prevent recurrent DKA in type 1 diabetes.
- **Lispro 6 units with meals + correction scale:** coordinate with actual meal intake; hold prandial dose if not eating per protocol but continue basal.
- **Potassium repletion:** monitor BMP; insulin shifts potassium intracellularly and can cause hypokalemia.
- **Carb-consistent diet:** mismatched intake and insulin can cause hypo- or hyperglycemia.
- **Recent insulin drip discontinuation:** verify overlap/timing with basal insulin to reduce rebound hyperglycemia.

### Decision triggers

- **If glucose persistently > 300 mg/dL**, check BMP/anion gap and consider ketones/VBG per protocol; call senior if gap reopening.
- **If anion gap reopens or bicarbonate drops**, treat as possible recurrent DKA and escalate promptly.
- **If glucose < 70 mg/dL**, treat hypoglycemia per protocol and reassess insulin/nutrition plan.
- **If potassium < 3.5 mEq/L**, replete and notify senior before giving large additional insulin doses depending on protocol.
- **If nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, tachypnea, or altered mental status develops**, repeat DKA labs and evaluate at bedside.

- BP may run lower than baseline while losartan and hydrochlorothiazide are held and intake recovers.
- Urine output should remain adequate; oliguria would be an important change.

### Medication-specific monitoring

- **Held hydrochlorothiazide:** do not restart overnight; avoid worsening volume depletion/electrolyte abnormalities.
- **Stopped maintenance fluids:** monitor intake, urine output, BP, edema/crackles, and need for small bolus.

- **If new crackles, hypoxemia, edema, or JVP elevation occurs after fluids**, stop fluids and evaluate for volume overload.
- **If morning creatinine rises from 1.9 or potassium is significantly abnormal**, continue holding ARB/thiazide and discuss further AKI workup/management.
